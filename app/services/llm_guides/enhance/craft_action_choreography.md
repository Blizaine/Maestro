ACTION SCENE CRAFT — CHOREOGRAPHY

Direct the fight as connected physical decisions, not a catalogue of impressive
move names. Preserve the user's fighters, abilities, choreography, restrictions,
tone and ending. A detailed script supplies the sequence; clarify its execution
without replacing it. For an open concept, author the missing exchanges below.

Give each fighter an observable strategy and a reason to change it: for example,
one closes distance with compact counters while the other controls range with
long attacks. Show these differences in their actions, not just in a style label.
Both read, defend and counter; neither waits for a turn as a passive target.
Do not assign an established ability to the wrong fighter or invent an ability
to solve a difficult transition.

Establish a simple arena with a few usable landmarks and both fighters' starting
positions. Build each important exchange as one causal chain: planted support
or wind-up, attack direction and target, contact or an explicit defense, then
the resulting balance, distance and position. The next attack exploits that
result. When someone is thrown, track the trajectory, collision and recovery
before they attack again. A miss or counter can be as dramatic as a clean hit.

Choose a few developed exchanges that fit the available seconds. Spend the
screen time on readable attack and response instead of squeezing a long flurry,
throw, flight and landing into a tiny phase. Do not simply add more punches to
make the prompt longer. Use brief anticipation and changing initiative when
the brief permits; honor continuous pressure or exact timing when specified.
Never add slow motion unless requested.

Make scale visible through directional consequences: a planted foot cracks its
support, a missed blow breaks what lies along its path, an impact displaces a
body and debris in the same direction. Establish obstacles before using them;
carry their damage and the fighters' resulting positions into later exchanges.
Keep requested effects at their physical source. Unaided flight uses body
posture, fabric and displaced air without exhaust; a beam cannot be caught or
redirected by an ordinary hand without an explicitly supplied ability.

Frame the contact and counter clearly. Track displacement to prove its distance;
use a wide view when changed geography matters. Camera movement accompanies an
exchange rather than becoming a separate event or a tour of the aftermath.

Escalate through a change of tactic, a reversal or a harder use of the arena.
Pay off the final exchange visibly and end in the position it caused. If the
user leaves the ending open, choose one consistent with the fight's established
contrast. Do not spend the final third on posing, recovering or surveying dust
while the requested combat has already stopped. Preserve a supplied restrained,
unresolved or continuing ending instead of forcing victory or extra destruction.
