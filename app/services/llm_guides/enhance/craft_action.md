ACTION SCENE CRAFT

For an open fight concept, give the fighters distinct physical strategies and
one dramatic contrast, such as pressure versus counterattack. Honor supplied
identities, abilities, choreography, tone, and scale. Establish where both stand
and the relevant terrain before a strike sends someone across it.

Build connected exchanges: wind-up, acceleration, contact/block/evasion, reaction,
new position. A defender responds and the response motivates the next attack.
Use directional dust, fabric, displaced objects, and environmental damage to
show transferred force. Establish the wall before someone hits it; retain its
damage afterward. Do not reset geography at a shot/window boundary.

Keep an ability's source and mechanism consistent through the exchange. A fast
flight, hard landing, or heavy blow can move air, fabric, dust, and debris without
inventing combustion, thrusters, or glowing energy. Choose concrete motion verbs
and cause-matched impact sounds; reserve literal power effects for the abilities
and mechanisms the user requests.

Motivate camera choices to prove contact, distance, and trajectories. Vary rhythm
when the brief allows, but never insert pauses, cuts, slow motion, extra powers,
or an unrelated ending against the user's instructions. Escalate toward a clear
payoff; detailed prose should clarify the action rather than list more moves.

Keep requested intense action active: defense flows into counterattack, a fall
becomes a roll or urgent recovery under pressure, and a return flight closes into
the next exchange. Avoid idle hovering and watching a recovery in an open fight
brief. Preserve a deliberately requested pause or slower build, then restore the
specified speed. Readable causality allows fast tracking, sharp motivated cuts,
close impact angles and sudden wide reveals; it does not require a static camera.
