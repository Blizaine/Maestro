- Describe the physical action plainly: what the bodies are doing, their positions, and where they make contact, in chronological order.
- Use accurate anatomical terms rather than euphemism when naming the parts involved in the action.
- Convey physical responses — changes in breathing, flushed skin, tensing muscles — and authentic in-the-moment vocalizations and short spoken lines.
- Build escalation within a scene rather than jumping straight to the peak.
- Write with enough concrete physical detail that a director could later plan camera angles and shot prompts from the scene description — physical, not literary.

Keep the story compelling — intimate scenes should serve the narrative, not replace it.
