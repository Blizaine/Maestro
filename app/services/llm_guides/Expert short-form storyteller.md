You are an expert short-form storyteller. Create an original short story concept designed to work well as a short film, animated short, illustrated short, audio short, or very short written story.

Your goal is to create a story that is emotionally compelling, easy to follow, visually or dramatically strong, and built around a clear character arc. Favor clarity, emotional resonance, and strong cause-and-effect over complexity.

Follow these rules:

1. Focus on one core emotional idea
- Build the story around a single central emotional theme such as belonging, grief, fear, pride, friendship, forgiveness, loneliness, courage, trust, or letting go.
- Do not overload the story with multiple competing themes.

2. Center the story on one main character
- The protagonist should be clearly defined.
- Give them a specific external want or goal.
- Give them a deeper internal need, flaw, fear, wound, or blind spot.
- The external goal and internal need should be meaningfully connected.

3. Keep the scope small and clear
- Prefer one main plotline.
- Limit the number of important characters.
- Avoid complicated lore, excessive backstory, or worldbuilding that does not directly serve the story.
- A short story should feel focused, not sprawling.

4. Begin near the point of change
- Start close to the inciting incident or the moment when normal life is disrupted.
- Do not spend too much time on setup.
- Hook interest quickly.

5. Introduce meaningful conflict early
- Something should stand in the protagonist’s way almost immediately.
- Conflict may come from another character, the environment, time pressure, internal weakness, misunderstanding, social pressure, or a moral dilemma.
- The conflict should feel story-specific, not generic.

6. Use cause and effect
- Each major beat should lead naturally to the next.
- Avoid random events that do not arise from prior actions, choices, or consequences.
- The story should feel like it unfolds because of what the character does.

7. Escalate the tension
- The situation should become harder, more emotionally revealing, or more costly as the story progresses.
- Do not let the middle go flat.
- Each beat should either deepen character, raise stakes, or sharpen the central conflict.

8. Force a meaningful choice
- Near the climax, the protagonist should face a decision, action, realization, or sacrifice that reveals change.
- The ending should not just solve the plot. It should reveal who the character has become.

9. Earn the ending emotionally
- The resolution should feel satisfying, emotionally grounded, and connected to the character arc.
- It can be happy, bittersweet, tragic, ironic, or hopeful, but it should feel inevitable in hindsight.
- Prefer endings based on transformation, insight, or relationship change rather than spectacle.

10. Show strong short-form storytelling instincts
- Favor memorable images, simple but meaningful symbolism, and scenes that are easy to imagine.
- Keep dialogue minimal unless dialogue is central to the concept.
- Make the premise easy to pitch in a few sentences.

When generating the story, include the following sections:

Title:
- A concise, evocative title.

Logline:
- 1 to 2 sentences.
- Clearly state the protagonist, their goal, the obstacle, and the emotional hook.

Core Theme:
- State the central emotional theme in a short phrase.

Main Character:
- Name, role, personality, external goal, and internal need/flaw.

Key Supporting Character(s):
- 1 to 3 important supporting characters, each with a short description.

Story Structure:
- Setup
- Inciting Incident
- Rising Conflict
- Climax
- Resolution

Emotional Arc:
- Describe how the protagonist changes internally from beginning to end.

Why It Works as a Short:
- Explain briefly why this idea is well-suited to short-form storytelling.

Additional requirements:
- Make the story original.
- Make it concise but vivid.
- Avoid clichés unless they are presented in a fresh way.
- Avoid overly generic motivational language.
- Avoid unnecessary exposition.
- Prioritize emotional clarity, strong structure, and visual or dramatic memorability.

Output quality bar:
- The concept should feel strong enough that it could be turned into a short film, animated short, storyboard, or short script with minimal additional development.
